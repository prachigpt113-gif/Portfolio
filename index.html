01The flagship · one problem, followed all the way through

The LINX story: from a survey finding to a B2B product.

Most case studies show you a screen. This one shows you the whole chain of reasoning — a research finding that became a product thesis, a consumer redesign, and an enterprise extension. Read it in order. It's the closest thing to watching me work.

Chapter 1 · Research

Lost in the Learning Loop

An independent mixed-methods study on why career-driven learners abandon what they start. A 20-person survey, four moderated interviews, affinity mapping, four archetypes.

The finding that changed everything: the learning decision is an identity decision in disguise. What separated people who finished from people who drifted wasn't discipline or time : it was clarity about who they were becoming.

Chapter 2 · Consumer design

LINX — what if onboarding asked a better question?

A redesign of LinkedIn Learning's onboarding built directly on the research. Instead of "what do you want to learn?", LINX asks "who are you becoming by choosing this?" — three identity paths replacing the skill picker, each mapped to an archetype.

The detail I'm proudest of: Commitment Echo re-anchors the learner at Module 2, the exact drift point the research identified. The design choice is the research finding, made tangible.

UX research · redesign
What if LinkedIn Learning asked a better question?
What if onboarding asked who you're becoming, not what you want to learn?
UX research → redesign · Figma prototype · grounded in the Learning Loop study
The problem
LinkedIn Learning asks which skills you want to learn. That assumes you've already decided who you're becoming. For most learners, that decision hasn't been made, and the course sits untouched somewhere between Module 1 and Module 2.
The approach
Insert one screen before the recommendations. Three answers, each mapped to a research archetype. LINX then uses what LinkedIn already knows, your profile and your network, as an opening guess to confirm or correct, never new data. For the unsettled learner it asks the question that matters most: what happens if you don't pursue this right now?
The one move that mattered
"Which skills are you interested in learning?"
↓ becomes
"Before we build your plan, what's driving this for you?"
The result
A "Commitment Echo" resurfaces the learner's own reason at Module 2, the exact drift point the research identified. Not a streak reminder. A mirror.
3
identity paths replace the skill picker
Module 2
the measured drift point, re-anchored by design
NO new data
reuses LinkedIn's existing profile + network graph
A research-grounded design proposal, not a shipped feature with invented metrics.
The call
Treat the moment before enrolment as the design surface. One identity question creates a commitment no post-enrolment nudge can manufacture.
